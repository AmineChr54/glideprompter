[WALK TO CENTRE] [PAUSE]<br>
Three years ago, I got **completely lost** in a city I had lived in for a decade. [PAUSE]<br>
My phone had died. No map, no blue dot, no friendly voice telling me to turn left in two hundred metres.<br>
And for the first ten minutes, I was honestly a little scared. [BREATHE]<br>
Then something strange happened. I started to *look*.<br>
[SLIDE 2]<br>
I noticed a bakery I had walked past a hundred times without seeing. A mural on the side of a parking garage. An old man teaching his granddaughter to play chess on a folding table.<br>
I had been living in this city, but I had never really ==been there==.<br>
[SLIDE 3]<br>
Today I want to talk about why we should all get lost a little more often. [PAUSE]<br>
Here is the problem. We have become *incredibly* good at not getting lost.<br>
We optimise every route. We read reviews before we pick a café. We know the weather, the traffic and the waiting time before we leave the house.<br>
And that is useful. I am not here to tell you to throw your phone into a lake. [SMILE]<br>
But researchers who study navigation have found something worth thinking about.<br>
When we follow turn-by-turn directions, the parts of our brain that build mental maps get very quiet.<br>
We arrive, but we do not really know where we are. [PAUSE]<br>
[SLIDE 4]<br>
I think the same thing happens in the rest of our lives.<br>
We follow the recommended path through school, through work, through our weekends.<br>
We arrive at the next milestone, but we are not always sure how we got there, or whether we wanted to go there at all.<br>
[SLOW] Getting lost forces us to pay attention. [PAUSE]<br>
It turns a route into a place. It turns a schedule into an experience.<br>
[SLIDE 5]<br>
So after that day, I started a small experiment. Once a week, I go somewhere **without a plan**.<br>
I take a bus I have never taken. I get off at a stop that sounds interesting. I walk until something surprises me.<br>
I have found a tiny museum about the history of buttons. [SMILE]<br>
I have eaten the best soup of my life in a place with no sign on the door.<br>
And I have had more real conversations with strangers in one year than in the ten before it. [PAUSE]<br>
Because when you are lost, you have to ask. And when you ask, people help. [BREATHE]<br>
[SLIDE 6]<br>
Now, getting lost does not have to mean geography.<br>
You can get lost in a book outside your usual genre. In a hobby you are bad at. In a conversation where you do not already know the answer.<br>
The key is to leave a little room for the {orange}unexpected{/}.<br>
[SLIDE 7]<br>
So here is my challenge for you. [LOOK UP]<br>
Sometime this week, put your phone in your pocket, pick a direction, and walk for twenty minutes.<br>
No destination. No reviews. No blue dot.<br>
Notice one thing you have never noticed before. [PAUSE]<br>
You might find a bakery. You might find a mural. You might find a chess game.<br>
Or you might find a part of yourself that you had stopped looking for. [PAUSE]<br>
**Thank you.** [BOW]
